Teen Patti rules and hand rankings

How to play, every hand ranked from strongest to weakest, and the exact chance of being dealt each one.

The goal

Teen Patti ("three cards") is played by 3 to 6 players with a standard 52-card deck, no jokers. Each player is dealt three cards face down. The aim is to hold the highest-ranking three-card hand at showdown, or to make everyone else fold before then.

A quirk worth knowing: a Pure Sequence (48 possible hands) is actually rarer than a Trail (52 hands), yet the Trail still ranks higher. Teen Patti's ranking isn't ordered strictly by probability — this one pair of hands is the exception.

How a round plays out

Before the deal, everyone puts a fixed stake (the boot) into the pot. Players then take turns, choosing to play seen (after looking at their cards) or blind (without looking). On your turn you either bet to stay in, or fold. When only two players remain, either can call for a show, and the higher hand takes the pot.

Common questions

Does a Trail beat a Pure Sequence?

Yes. A Trail (three of a kind) is the highest hand in Teen Patti, above a Pure Sequence — even though a Pure Sequence is slightly rarer.

Is A-2-3 a valid sequence?

Yes. A-2-3 is a valid run, and in most house rules it is the second-highest run after A-K-Q.

Which is higher between two flushes?

Compare the highest card; if tied, the next, then the last.
